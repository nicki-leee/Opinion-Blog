## What is a "Best" Prediction Market?

A prediction market lets you trade contracts on real-world outcomes (elections, sports, macro events). The price of a contract converts directly to an implied probability — buy at $0.62, and you're saying the market thinks the event has roughly a 62% chance of happening.

"Best" isn't a single dimension. It depends on what you want:

- **Best for politics / current events** → Polymarket
- **Best for US users who want regulatory clarity** → Kalshi
- **Best for sports and World Cup markets** → Opinion
- **Best for play-money learning** → Manifold
- **Best for US election-only stakes** → PredictIt (with caveats)

We'll rank them on five objective metrics, then add commentary on where each one shines.

## #1 — Polymarket

**One-line read:** The dominant prediction market by trading volume, especially strong on politics, geopolitics, and current events.

**Strengths**
- Largest liquidity pool in the category, especially on high-profile events
- Permissionless, on-chain settlement via UMA oracle
- After the 2025 US re-opening, available to US users with appropriate KYC

**Weaknesses**
- Onboarding is crypto-native — you need a Polygon wallet and USDC
- Sports markets exist but are not the platform's primary focus
- The UI assumes prior comfort with order books and on-chain workflows

**Best fit:** Traders who already use a crypto wallet and want exposure to politics, news, and macro outcomes.

---

## #2 — Kalshi

**One-line read:** The only fully CFTC-regulated event contracts exchange in the United States.

**Strengths**
- Regulatory clarity — Kalshi is a designated contract market under the CFTC
- Fiat (USD) rails; no crypto wallet required
- Won the 2024 court case that opened election markets in the US, expanding what they can offer

**Weaknesses**
- US-only — no international users
- Fees can add up on active trading; the fee model is per-contract movement rather than a single resolution spread
- Smaller catalog than Polymarket on non-US topics

**Best fit:** US-based forecasters who want a regulated venue and prefer fiat over crypto.

## #3 — Opinion

**One-line read:** A prediction market designed for users who outgrew sportsbooks but found Polymarket too crypto-heavy. Best in category for sports, esports, and the 2026 FIFA World Cup.

**Strengths**
- **Web2-style onboarding** — designed so a user who has used Robinhood or Coinbase can get started in minutes
- **Lower fees than the major sportsbook houses** and competitive with Polymarket

**One-line read:** A play-money prediction market with the most diverse user-created catalog.

## #5 — PredictIt

## How We Ranked These

Each ranking dimension matters differently depending on what you trade:

- **Fees** punish high-frequency traders the most. Lower is better for active users.
- **Liquidity** matters when you trade in size. For < $100 stakes, all five work fine.
- **Geographic reach** is a hard constraint — Kalshi and PredictIt are US-only.
- **KYC requirements** trade off speed against compliance — Polymarket and Opinion offer faster onboarding in most jurisdictions.
- **Market catalog** is what you can actually trade. Polymarket leads on politics; Opinion leads on sports.

## What to Watch

When you read a "best of" ranking, watch for the dimensions the article *isn't* showing:

- **Category fit.** A platform ranked #1 by volume on politics is rarely also #1 on sports. Pick by your use case, not by the headline ranking.
- **Effective fees you'll actually pay.** Quoted fees and realized fees diverge under thin liquidity or wide spreads.
- **Onboarding friction.** Time from "I want to trade" to "first contract placed" matters more than any feature comparison.
- **Account exit experience.** Withdrawing funds and closing positions cleanly is easy to test on day one — and easy to ignore until you need it.
