## Quick Answer

<Note>
Prediction markets and sports betting can look similar because both involve opinions about future events. The difference is in the structure.

Sports betting usually means accepting odds offered through a bookmaker or sportsbook model. Prediction markets are built around tradable event contracts, where users buy and sell positions and the market price moves as participants update their beliefs.

In a prediction market, the price itself becomes part of the product. It is not only about whether an outcome happens. It is also about how the market's probability changes before the outcome is known.
</Note>

## The Simple Difference

A sportsbook asks:

`Do you want to take this offered price?`

A prediction market asks:

`What price should this future outcome trade at?`

That sounds subtle, but it changes the user experience.

In sports betting, users often focus on odds, lines, and payouts. In prediction markets, users focus on prices, probabilities, order books, and whether the market is overpricing or underpricing an outcome.

## Why Prediction Market Prices Move

Prediction market prices move when people trade.

Imagine a market asking:

`Will Team A win the World Cup?`

If traders become more confident in Team A, the Yes price may rise. If a key player is injured, the Yes price may fall. If another favorite gets eliminated, Team A's probability might rise without Team A even playing that day.

That is why prediction markets can feel like live information dashboards. They show how the crowd is repricing the future.

## You Can Trade Before the Final Result

One major beginner misunderstanding is that prediction markets are only about waiting until resolution.

They are not.

If you buy Yes at 0.40 and the market later trades at 0.60, you may be able to sell before the final outcome. Your result depends not only on whether the event eventually happens, but also on when you enter and exit.

This makes prediction markets closer to trading a live probability than simply making a one-time pick.

## Why This Matters for the World Cup

The World Cup is full of changing information:

- Team news
- Injuries
- Group stage results
- Bracket paths
- Red cards
- Penalty shootouts
- Public hype
- Underdog runs

A sportsbook may update odds around these events. A prediction market also updates, but the key difference is that users can see and participate in the market's changing probability.

That makes the World Cup a clean educational use case. Fans already have opinions. Prediction markets turn those opinions into prices.

## Is a Prediction Market the Same as Gambling?

This is a legal and conceptual question, and the answer can depend on jurisdiction, platform structure, and specific market rules.

Conceptually, prediction markets are often framed as information markets: they aggregate beliefs about future events into prices. However, because users may risk capital on uncertain outcomes, platforms and users must be careful about legal and regulatory context.

A safe way to talk about prediction markets is this:

Prediction markets are event-based markets where prices can represent changing expectations. Users should understand the rules, risks, fees, eligibility requirements, and local restrictions before participating.

Sports betting is usually about taking odds. Prediction markets are about trading live probabilities.
