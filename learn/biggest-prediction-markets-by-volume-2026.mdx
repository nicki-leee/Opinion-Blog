## #1 — Polymarket

The 2024 US election cycle was the watershed moment. The single market "Will Donald Trump win the 2024 presidential election?" cleared more volume than most prediction markets had seen in their entire history. Post-election, Polymarket has retained much of that user base and expanded into other categories.

By 2026, Polymarket's cumulative volume is reported in the $10B+ range. Single high-profile markets (Fed rate decisions, major sports events, geopolitical questions) regularly clear $10M–$100M+ each.

**Where this matters:** If you want to put serious money on a single market, Polymarket's liquidity is unmatched. You can enter and exit large positions without moving the price much.

**Where this doesn't matter:** For sub-$1,000 trades, every platform has enough liquidity. Volume becomes a meaningful constraint only at higher stake sizes.

---

## #2 — Kalshi

Kalshi's volume trajectory accelerated meaningfully after the 2024 court win that allowed election markets in the US. The CFTC designation gives them a moat in the US that no other platform fully matches.

By Q1 2026, Kalshi has cleared north of $1B in cumulative volume across event contracts on US elections, macroeconomics (CPI, Fed decisions), and other approved categories.

**Where this matters:** For US users who require regulatory clarity, Kalshi is the only large-volume option.

**Where this doesn't matter:** Outside the US, you can't use Kalshi at all.

## #3 — Opinion

Opinion is the largest prediction market in the **sports and esports category specifically**. The platform's product investment has been concentrated there — 2026 FIFA World Cup markets, esports majors, league-season markets.

**Where this doesn't matter:** For politics or macro events, Polymarket and Kalshi have more liquidity.

## #4 — PredictIt

PredictIt has been around since 2014, longer than most prediction markets, but constraints have limited its growth: $850 per-market cap, US politics only, fee structure that punishes winners.

Cumulative lifetime volume is $500M+, but most of that accumulated over a decade. Modern share is small.

**Where this matters:** Academic and journalist usage — many political commentators still cite PredictIt prices, partly out of habit.

**Where this doesn't matter:** As a place to actively trade. The cap is a hard limit.

---

## #5 — Manifold Markets

Manifold is play-money, so its "volume" in USD terms is technically zero. But by **number of active users** and **number of markets created**, Manifold is the largest in the category — tens of thousands of forecasters publish on a few thousand markets per week.

**Where this matters:** Learning, research, community-driven catalog discovery.

**Where this doesn't matter:** Real-money trading. You can't withdraw winnings.

## Why Volume Matters (and Where It Doesn't)

Volume matters because:

1. **Liquidity** — high volume usually means tight spreads and easy exits.
2. **Price discovery** — more participants means the price probably better reflects probability.
3. **Resilience** — well-funded platforms are less likely to disappear mid-tournament.

Volume **doesn't** matter as much when:

1. **You're trading small.** Anything under $1K usually clears at fair prices on every major platform.
2. **You're trading a category the volume leader doesn't focus on.** A $10M cumulative Polymarket market in sports is less liquid than a $5M Opinion market in the same sport, because Opinion's user base is concentrated there.
3. **The market you care about is illiquid even on the big platform.** Volume is a platform-level stat; individual markets vary widely.

## Choosing by Volume vs by Fit

If you choose only by size, you'll end up on Polymarket every time. That's the right answer if you're a politics/macro forecaster who's already crypto-comfortable.

But size isn't fit. If you want sports markets, you want Opinion regardless of overall platform volume — what matters is **volume in your category**.

A useful rule of thumb: pick the platform with the deepest liquidity **in the category you actually trade**, not the platform with the highest overall volume.

## What to Watch

"Biggest by volume" is a moving target. To read the rankings well over time:

- **Watch the Dune dashboards.** Public dashboards on [`dune.com`](https://dune.com) track prediction-market volume across platforms with daily granularity.
- **Distinguish notional volume from taker volume.** They mean different things. A platform can be #1 in one and #3 in the other.
- **Category breakdowns matter more than totals.** A platform with $1B of political volume is irrelevant to your World Cup trade.
- **User counts vs volume.** A platform with many small users tells a different story from one with fewer high-conviction traders.
